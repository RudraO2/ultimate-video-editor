# Working in Ultimate Video Editor

Ultimate Video Editor is a desktop motion design and video editor. A project holds **comps**, also called **frames** (each a timeline with its own size, frame rate and length, or a static picture), kept on **pages**, and a **media** list. A comp holds **tracks**, and each track holds **layers**: text, shape, image, video or audio. Every property can be keyframed. You drive it through tools; the user sees every change live in the editor and can undo your edits.

Aim for work that looks intentional: one focal point, clear type, motion with a reason.

## The loop

1. **Orient.** `doc_overview` first: pages, comps with their sizes and modes, media, and where the user is (`editor.openCompId`, playhead, selection). Then `layers_list` for the comp you will touch.
2. **Plan once.** For anything an audience will watch (an ad, a launch film, a reel, an explainer), find the story first and write a beat sheet: `uve://skill/references/storytelling.md`. Then the layers, their layout and the timing, before you create anything; a recipe close to the request is a good start (`uve://skill/references/recipes.md`). Plan the craft too, not only the layout: per-unit entrances (`animation_set`) instead of whole-layer fades, sound (a bed under anything over 3 s, and a sound on the beats that matter: `sounds` on the layer, see "Sounds on motion" and "The mix"), a transition on each cut that needs one (`transition_add`), one effect where it earns its place (a soft shadow, a glow, a shine on a reveal: `effects_add`), and word-synced captions when there are words to follow (`caption_set`). Motion blur is already on. Take sizes, margins, colours and timings from the design system (`uve://skill/design-systems/default/DESIGN.md`, unless the user gave a brand), and start type from a text template when one fits (`catalog_list text-templates`).
3. **Build in batches.** One `api_batch` per idea (a card and its text, the keys of an entrance). Inside a batch, `"$0.id"` is the id returned by call 0. Anything repetitive or computed (a grid, a stagger over many words, a layout from data, or the whole scene once the plan is fixed) is one `script_run`: see "Scripts".
4. **Look.** `view_render` after a visible change, `view_frames` for anything that moves. Use `detail: "glance"` for layout, `"look"` by default, `"inspect"` with `layers` to check type up close.
5. **Refine.** Fix what you saw, then look again.
6. **Sound.** Required for any piece that plays, and done after the picture is right, so the beats are final. In order: (a) a bed: a music file the user gave or the project has (`media_list`; the CC0 library has none; with none, skip it and say so), cut to the comp, faded in and out; (b) routing: every media clip has its category (`soundBus`), and the leveler stays on; (c) a sound on each beat that matters, on the layer that causes it, `hitMs` on the frame of impact, one family; (d) the voice, if any, is the loudest thing. Details in "The mix" and "Sounds on motion". Do not hand-ride clip volumes to balance things.
7. **Finish.** When the brief asks for a video, `export_video` after a last `view_frames`, and read `loudness` in its reply: integrated −16 to −14 LUFS and `truePeakDbtp` at or below −1 is right. Far off means a cause to fix (a missing bed, an untagged clip, a muted category), not a clip gain to nudge. `project_save` when the brief asks to keep the project.
8. **Report.** Tell the user what you made in two or three sentences, with the file paths. `report_issue` anything you could not do.

## Coordinates, time, names

- Comp space has its **origin at the comp centre, +y down**. A 1920×1080 comp spans x −960…960 and y −540…540. `x`, `y` place the layer's **pivot**, which is its centre unless you move it.
- The pivot (`anchorX`, `anchorY`, or AtomCut's `anchor.x`, `anchor.y`, or `anchor: [ax, ay]`) is a fraction of the layer's own box: 0.5 is the centre, 0 the left or top edge, 1 the right or bottom edge. Rotation and scale turn around it. For a left-aligned stack, give every line `anchorX: 0` and the same `x`: the left edges line up exactly and each line grows to the right, whatever its words. On `layer_create` the pivot goes inside `transform` (`transform: {anchorX: 0}`); `layer_set` takes `anchorX` by name.
- `z` is depth in pixels (positive is away from the viewer); at `z = 0` the picture is pixel-exact. `rotationX` leans the top away, `rotationY` turns the right side away, `rotation` is clockwise, in degrees.
- A comp with `world` is a 3D world and its axes differ (Z is up): see "3D worlds" before touching `x`, `y`, `z` there.
- Times are **milliseconds**. `startMs` and `durationMs` are comp time. Keyframe times are **relative to the layer start** unless you pass `clock: "comp"`.
- Tracks are ordered front (index 0) to back. Layers on one track never overlap; `layer_create` without `trackId` makes a new track in front.
- Address layers by id, or by a name only one layer has. Name things well.
- Tool names use `_` (`layer_set`); inside `api_batch` the command names use `.` (`layer.set`). Both work in either place.

## Three levels of writing

Use the highest one that fits:

1. **Verbs** such as `layer_create` and `timeline_split` keep the rules (lanes, media bounds, ids).
2. **`layer_set`** for any animatable value: `x`, `y`, `z`, `rotation`, `rotationX`, `rotationY`, `scale` (both axes, or `[sx, sy]`), `position` (`[x, y]`), `scaleX`, `scaleY`, `anchorX`, `anchorY`, `opacity`; text `fontSize`, `color`, `letterSpacing`, `lineHeight`, `maxWidth`, `maxHeight`; shape `width`, `height`, `cornerRadius`; paints `fill`, `strokeColor`, `strokeWidth`, `trimStart`, `trimEnd`, `trimOffset` (the first fill and stroke) or any paint by AtomCut's path (`fill.<paintId>`, `fill.<paintId>.opacity`, `fill.<paintId>.stop.<stopId>.color`, `stroke.<strokeId>.width`, `stroke.<strokeId>.trim.end`, …); media `volume`. `layer_properties` lists a layer's properties with values and ranges. Once a property has keys, writing it adds a key at `atMs` (comp time; default the playhead). `mode: "keyframe"` always keys; `mode: "base"` sets the resting value.
3. **`layer_patch`** for everything else, such as `style.fontFamily`, `style.fontWeight`, `style.align`, `text`, `shape.type`, `name`, `enabled`.

Inputs are strict: an unknown field is an error that names it. When unsure, call `api_describe {"command": "layer.create"}`.

## Paints: fills and strokes

Shape and text layers have ordered `fills` and `strokes` (bottom to top, strokes over fills), like AtomCut and Figma.

- A paint is a colour (`"#ff3366"`, `#rrggbbaa` for alpha) or `{type: "linear" | "radial" | "angular", stops: [{offset, color}], start, end}`. `start` and `end` are points of the layer's box as `{x, y}` (0–1): a linear gradient runs from `start` to `end`; a radial one is centred on `start` and reaches `end`; an angular one turns around `start`. Each paint has `opacity` and `blendMode`.
- `layer_create` takes `fill` and `stroke` (one each, `null` for none) or `fills` and `strokes` (lists). A stroke is a colour (4 px, centred), `{color, width}`, or `{paint, width, align: center | inside | outside, cap, join, dash: [length, gap], dashOffset, trim: {start, end, offset}}`. Text with no fills draws in `style.color`; a fill replaces it (gradient type).
- Change paints later with `layer_patch` (`insert` into `fills[-]` or `strokes[-]`; `set` on `fills[id=<paintId>].stops`); ids are minted for you, and keys of a removed paint are dropped and listed. `layer_properties` lists every paint property path.
- **Draw-on:** key `trimEnd` (or `stroke.<id>.trim.end`) from 0 to 1, ease-in-out, 800–1500 ms. A rectangle's outline starts at its top-left corner and an ellipse's at the top, both clockwise; `trimOffset` (laps) moves the start. Trim needs an outline, so text strokes cannot be trimmed.
- **Soft light behind a title:** an ellipse with a radial fill from the colour at 60–70 % alpha to the same colour at 0. **Outline text:** an `outside` stroke on a text layer, or a `stroke` layer effect (it follows the drawn pixels, so it also outlines images and groups).

## Shapes, paths and masks

- Shapes: `layer_create {kind: "shape", shape: "rect" | "ellipse" | "polygon" | "star", width, height, cornerRadius, pointCount, innerRatio}`. `innerRatio` (a star's inner radius) can be keyed.
- **Paths:** `layer_create {kind: "path", d: "<SVG path data>", fill, stroke}` draws any outline (`M`, `L`, `H`, `V`, `C`, `S`, `Q`, `T`, `A`, `Z`). Write `d` in any space: the layer is centred on the outline's box, so place it with `x` and `y`. A path with a fill and no stroke has no outline; a bare one gets a 4 px light line. `fillRule: "evenodd"` makes overlaps holes. One path layer is one piece of art (holes included); pieces that move or colour apart are separate layers.
- **Morph:** key `geometry` (each key's value is the `d` string itself) with `d` strings that have the same commands (for example eight `C` segments each); they blend point by point, and the layer keeps the box of its resting outline so the pivot does not drift. Paths with different commands switch at the next key.
- **Draw-on** works on paths too (`trimEnd` 0 → 1); each piece of a compound path draws on at the same time.
- **Masks and cut-outs:** group the pieces and set the group's `mode` (`layers_group {ids, mode}` or `layer_patch` `mode`): `mask` shows the others only inside the stencil (a shine band clipped to a logo, a reveal through a shape), `subtract` cuts the stencil out, `intersect` keeps the overlap, `exclude` keeps where only one is, `union` draws the members as one picture. The stencil is the member with `isStencil: true` (several are combined), or the front-most member when none is marked; stencils are not drawn themselves. Animate the stencil to make wipes and reveals.

## Icons and SVG

- `icons_list {query: "arrow right"}` searches every set (Lucide, Material Symbols, Tabler, Phosphor, Solar, Fluent, emoji, logos, flags and about 225 more: 413,000 icons); `set: "lucide"` narrows it to one set (a prefix or part of its name), `style` is `line` (drawn with strokes), `solid` (filled) or `color` (logos, emoji, flags in their own colours). With no query it lists the sets. Search by one or two plain words ("camera", "heart", "arrow"); an icon id is `set:name`.
- `icons_use {id: "lucide:house", x, y, size: 160, color: "#F4F4FA", animIn: "draw"}` places it as vector layers: a path layer, or several grouped when the icon has parts painted differently (a logo, an emoji). `size` is the longer side in px, `x`, `y` the centre in comp space, `color` is what `currentColor` stands for (icons in their own colours keep them), `strokeWidth` overrides the line width of a line icon (in the icon units: 2 on a 24 grid). `animIn: "draw"` draws the lines on, and works on solid icons too; any other animation preset animates the icon as one piece. The icon is made of real paths: recolour it with `layer_set` on `fill` or `strokeColor`, or key its `strokes[…].trim` to draw it.
- Credit: the layers remember where the icon came from. `doc_overview` lists `credits` (sounds and icon sets in use, with their licences); some licences (Creative Commons Attribution) ask for a credit line in the finished video or its description, and `icons_use` says so when it applies. Tell the user.
- `svg_place {svg: "<svg viewBox=…>…</svg>", size, x, y, color}` places any SVG markup the same way. Paths, rectangles, circles, ellipses, lines, polylines and polygons with their fills, strokes (caps, joins, dashes) and gradients are drawn; masks, clip paths, filters, text and images are not, and the result lists what was left out. Neighbouring outlines painted alike join into one layer.
- Icons come from an online library and are kept on this computer once fetched; one never seen cannot be fetched offline (the error says so).

## Stickers and pictures

- Stickers carry what a word cannot: a reaction (party popper, fire, mind blown), an object the line names (rocket, coffee, money bag), a hand that points or claps. One per beat, in time with the line or the sound that names it; a scatter of five at once is noise. Icons are for interface marks and line drawings; stickers are for warmth and character.
- One look per piece: all `3d` (soft rendered, the default and the most alive in motion), all `flat` (colour vectors, crisp at any size) or all `drawn` (black-line people and scenes from Open Peeps and Open Doodles, for a light ground). Never mix 3D and flat emoji in one video.
- `stickers_list {query: "party popper", style: "3d"}`: search by what it shows in one or two plain words (`heart`, `thumbs up`, `rocket`, `clapping`); every word must match, so a long phrase finds nothing; `#tag` requires a tag (`#drawn`). Results give `id`, `name`, `style`, the emoji `glyph` and tags; `count` says how many matched, `offset` pages.
- `stickers_use {id, place: {x, y, size: 220, startMs, durationMs}}` adds the sticker to the project with its credit and places it as an image layer centred at `x`, `y`; `size` is its long side in px. A 3D sticker is a 256 px picture: keep it at most about 300 px on screen (it softens when larger); flat and drawn ones are vectors and stay sharp at any size. Without `place` it is only added to Media; `layer_create {kind: "media", assetId}` places it later, and the same sticker used twice is one media item.
- Give every sticker an entrance and a sound: `animation_set {id, slot: "in", preset: "pop-in"}` (its overshoot is the point; `bounce-in` or `drop` for something that lands), an exit quicker than the entrance, and a `pop` voice on its own sounds stack (see "Sounds on motion"). Stagger a group 30 to 80 ms apart, each with its own pop. A sticker that sits still for seconds can `breathe` or rock gently.
- `media_remove_background {id}` makes a picture's flat background transparent (a product on white, a key green or magenta): on an image layer the layer switches to the cut-out and keeps its transform, timing and keys; on a media id the cut-out joins Media (`place` puts it on the timeline). Edges stay soft and lose the ground colour. It refuses a photo with a real background (this is a colour key, not an AI model: say so and `report_issue` it) and video (add the `chromaKey` effect instead). On white, holes inside the subject (eyes, highlights) are kept; `holes: true` clears them too.
- Credit: Fluent Emoji (Microsoft) is MIT; Open Peeps and Open Doodles are CC0. `doc_overview` lists credits; no credit line is required, but say in the report which libraries you used.

## Physics

Reach for it when things should fall, pile up, collide, swing or blow away: confetti and sticker rain, a title that drops onto a shelf, a logo that bounces in, dominoes, a hanging sign. Keys are better for designed motion (a move to a pose, an entrance); physics is better when many things interact and hand-keyed motion would look stiff.

- **Bodies.** `physics_body {ids, type}`: `active` falls and collides, `passive` is a fixed collider (a headline things land on, a shelf, a ramp; it can be at opacity 0), `animated` moves by its keys and shoves active bodies. `type: null` removes. Each collides on what it is (`shape: "auto"`): a picture on its silhouette (a sticker lands on its edge, not its box), text on each line's ink, a rectangle as a box, an ellipse as a circle, a star or path on its outline. `box`, `sphere`, `hull` override. Only top-level layers and whole groups can be bodies (a group falls as one); a member of a group is refused.
- **The stage.** `physics_scene {walls: true}`: the comp's bottom edge is a floor by default (`floorAt` moves it, `floor: false` drops it), walls keep everything in frame and still let things fall in from above. `gravity` 981 is Earth (0 floats), `startMs` is when physics takes over, `timeScale` < 1 is slow motion.
- **Time.** Before `startMs` a body sits where its keys put it; from then on the simulation moves it, starting with the speed its keys had (key a throw, start physics at its end, and it flies on). A body enters at its clip's start and leaves at its end: when a shelf's clip ends, what rests on it falls. `velocity` [x, y] (px/s, +y down) and `spin` [0, 0, deg/s] (+ clockwise) push on top.
- **Material.** `bounce` 0.6–0.8 rubber, 0.2–0.3 wood or paper, 0 lands dead; `friction` 0.6+ stops sliding; `mass` is relative; `damping` is air; `follow` 0.1–0.3 pulls a body back to its own keys with a jiggle (a bouncy badge), 0.8+ holds it firmly.
- **Forces.** `physics_force {type: "wind", strength, rotation}` makes a carrier layer (wind blows along its +X: `rotation: 90` blows down, `-20` up and right); `id` adds the force to a layer that exists. Strength is an acceleration (gravity is 981): wind 300 a breeze, 800 a gale; `point` attracts (negative repels), `vortex` swirls, `turbulence` wanders (`scale` px), `drag` slows (% per second). `radius` limits the reach. **A gust is keys on `force.<forceId>.strength`** (`keyframes_set`: 0 → 700 → 0 over 0.3 to 0.6 s, on a beat). Forces add up; `effects_update {id, effectId: forceId}` edits, mutes or removes a card.
- **Joints.** `physics_joint {id, add: [{type: "pin", anchor: [0, -h/2, 0]}]}` hangs a body from the comp at its top centre (a sign, a pendulum); `target` joins two bodies; `weld` makes one piece, `rope` stays within `length`, `spring` pulls back (`stiffness`, `damping`). Anchors are in the body's own px from its pivot.
- **No baking needed.** The simulation is part of the document: every frame, scrubbed, looped or exported, shows the same result, and any edit re-runs it. `physics_bake {ids?}` turns active bodies into a few eased keys (x, y, rotation) and removes their bodies, only when the user wants to hand-edit the motion.
- **Check it.** `view_frames` across the fall (look for bodies leaving the frame, sinking into each other, or a pile that never settles: add walls, lower `bounce`, add `friction` or `damping`), and `physics_inspect {atMs}` at the impact and the settle for positions.

**Sticker rain (the recipe).** About 20 stickers of one look (`stickers_use` with `place`, `size` 150–210, `durationMs` the whole comp), above the frame at staggered heights (y from just above the top edge up to two frames higher, x spread across), each `physics_body` with its own small random `spin` (±90) and `velocity` x (±150), `bounce` 0.3, `friction` 0.6; the headline `passive` so they pile on its letters; `physics_scene {walls: true}`; a wind carrier at strength 0 keyed to about 700 for 0.3 s on a beat for a gust; about 1 s of settle before the cut; a few soft impact sounds on the first landings, not one per sticker. A falling title is the same with text: one text layer per word (or letter), `bounce` 0.25, a passive line as the shelf.

## Characters

Reach for it when a person, mascot or face should feel alive on screen: a host who speaks the voiceover, a mascot reacting, an emoji that talks. A character that sits still reads as clip art; one that blinks, breathes and nods with its words reads as a performer.

- **Art in parts.** Life needs separate layers. Place a drawn person or a flat emoji as layers: `stickers_use {id, layers: true, place: {x, y, size, startMs, durationMs}}` (Open Peeps people and flat Fluent faces; a 3D sticker is one picture and can only breathe and nod as a whole) or `svg_place {svg, groups: true}` for your own SVG; the result's `groupId` is the character. Keep the character on screen for the whole line.
- **Read it first.** `character_detect {id}` lists the parts it found (head, face, eye.L/R, mouth, torso…; `.L` is the character's left, the screen's right) with a confidence and a reason, and `life` says what can be switched on or why not (`No eyes found`, `No voice: add a voice line to talk`). Wrong or missing parts: `overrides: {"mouth": layerId, "eye.R": null}` on either verb.
- **Bring it to life in one call.** `character_bringToLife {id}` (one undo step): a one-drawing face is split into its pieces; a Mouth switch (X rest, MBP, E, AI, O, U) talks with the voice layer that overlaps the character (`voice` names one); both eyes blink together about every 4 s; the body breathes (1.2 %, 16 a minute) on a Body bone; the head nods with the voice's loudness on a Head bone. `look: true` adds glances; `blinkEvery` changes the rhythm; `talk: false` (or any behaviour `false`) turns that one off; `dryRun: true` shows the plan and changes nothing (its `script` is the same plan as code: edit it and `script_run {code}` it); running it again adds nothing. Import the voice line BEFORE bringing the character to life, so talk and nod can hear it.
- **Behaviours are live, not keys.** They are a stack on the character (`behaviors_inspect {groupId}`; `types: true` lists every kind and field): `blink` (every, closedMs), `breathe` (amount %, rate per minute), `sway` (amount °, speed Hz: idle drift for a character with no voice), `nod` (amount ° at full loudness), `glance` (rangeX, rangeY px, hold s), `talk` (lead ms: the mouth moves that early). `behaviors_add`, `behaviors_update {fields | enabled}`, `behaviors_remove`. Same document, same frames: preview and export agree; another `seed` is another take.
- **Switches and rigs by hand.** A switch shows one member: `switch_create {ids | id, name}`, `switch_show {id, show: name | index, atMs, key: true}` (keys hold: a pose per beat). A rig is bones on a group whose pieces ride them whole: `rig_create {id, bones: [{name, head: [x, y], tail: [x, y], parent?}]}` (comp px; draw bones through the pieces they move, joint to joint), `rig_pose {rigId, atMs, key: true, poses: [{bone, rotation}]}` (degrees clockwise from rest), `rig_bone {rigId, edit: [{bone, ik: {target, chain: 2}, limits: {rotation: [0, 150]}}]}` for an arm that reaches for a control bone, `rig_bind` to pin a piece to a bone, `rig_inspect` to check. Art that bends along bones (one drawn arm) is not built yet: cut it into pieces.
- **Check it.** `view_frames` across the line, close on the face: the eyes shut together now and then (not in every frame), the mouth changes with the syllables and rests in the pauses, the head moves a little on loud words, the body never jumps.

**A talking host (the recipe).** Import the voice line first (`media_import {localPath, place: {startMs}}`, category Voice); place one person as layers (`stickers_use {layers: true}`, drawn people on a light ground, size 700–900 px, on one third of the frame); `character_bringToLife {id}`; check that `life` is all on and look with `view_frames`; put the line's words beside them as a caption timed to the speech; a quiet music bed ducked under the voice.

## Scripts

A script is JavaScript run against the open project in one call: every tool is a verb, `layer_create` is `await ac.layer.create({...})` with the same input and the same result. One `script_run` replaces tens of tool calls, and the whole run is one undo step.

**When.** Five or more similar calls (a grid, a stagger over forty words, one card per row of data); anything computed (positions on a circle, timings from a beat list, seeded randomness); and the whole build of a scene once its plan is fixed: layers, keys, entrances, effects and sounds in one run. One-off edits stay tool calls; look (`view_render`, `view_frames`) between runs, not inside them.

**The runtime.**
- The code is the body of an async function: top-level `await`, `return value` comes back as `value`. Globals: `ac`, `here`, `console`. Nothing else: no `fetch`, timers, files or imports.
- `here` is the comp it runs in (`{frameId, name, width, height, fps, durationMs, mode}`; `script_run {frameId}` picks another). Calls without a comp id land in it. Size and place from `here.width` and `here.height`, time from `here.durationMs`; never paste a comp id.
- `ac.help()` lists namespaces, `ac.help("layer")` the verbs, `ac.help("layer.create")` one verb's input. `ac.call("layer.create", input)` also works.
- **Await every call.** A failed call throws `CommandError` (message, `hint`, `line`); catch it only when failure is fine. Uncaught, the run stops and is **rolled back whole**: nothing changes, and the reply names the line, the hint and the last logs. The same happens past `timeoutMs` (default 30 s, at most 300 s) or on `script_stop`.
- `console.log` lines come back with their line numbers (the last 40 in the reply, all of them with `script_logs {runId}`).
- `Math.random` is seeded (`seed`, default 1) and `Date` is frozen, so the same script on the same project makes the same document. Another `seed` is another take.
- `script_run` and `api_batch` cannot be called inside a script.

**Saved scripts.** `script_save {path, source, onRun}` keeps a script in the project (the user sees it in the Scripts panel and can run it there); `script_run {script: path}` runs it. In `replace` mode (the default) a run first removes what its last run made, so edit, run, edit, run rebuilds and never stacks; `append` adds every time. Write a saved script as the whole recipe for its output: it creates everything it needs and sets values instead of incrementing them. Save one when the user will re-run or tweak it, with the knobs as constants at the top; one-offs use `script_run {code}`.

**Watching a long run.** `script_run` answers when the run ends. Meanwhile, from the same session, `script_runs` shows each run (calls made, the command it waits on, the last log), `script_logs {runId, since}` reads new lines, `script_stop {runId}` stops one.

**The recipe: a scene in one run.**
1. Plan as usual (layout grid, type scale, timing, sounds), then put the plan's numbers at the top of the script as constants.
2. Build everything in order, keeping ids from results: `const { id } = await ac.layer.create({...})`. Use the same verbs and shapes as the tools: `animation_set` presets for entrances, `keyframes_set` for poses (keys are poses, never a key per frame), `effects_add`, `sounds` on the layers that move, `caption_set`.
3. `return` what you will need next (ids by name) and `console.log` one line per part.
4. Look with `view_frames` and `view_render`, then fix with single tool calls, or edit the saved script and run it again in replace mode.

```js
const WORDS = ['Design', 'in', 'motion']
const SIZE = Math.round(here.height * 0.11)  // display size from the comp's short side
const STAGGER = 140                           // ms between words
const made = {}
for (const [i, word] of WORDS.entries()) {
  const { id } = await ac.layer.create({ kind: 'text', text: word, name: word, y: (i - 1) * SIZE * 1.15, style: { fontSize: SIZE } })
  await ac.animation.set({ id, slot: 'in', preset: 'fade-up', unit: 'letter', staggerMs: 18, delayMs: i * STAGGER })
  made[word] = id
}
console.log('words', Object.keys(made).length)
return made
```

Check the name with `fonts_list {query: "playfair"}`; a name that exists nowhere draws in a fallback face. `fonts_list` filters by `category` (sans, serif, display, handwriting, mono), `feeling` (Calm, Business, Playful, Sophisticated, Futuristic, Vintage, Loud…), `style` (Geometric, Humanist, Didone…), `variable` and `italic`, and says each family weights (a variable family covers a range).

## Groups and auto layout

- `layers_group {ids, name}` wraps layers (all top-level, or all in the same group) in a group. The group's transform is a change from where its members are: key its `x`, `y`, `scale`, `rotation` (around a point of its box, set by its pivot) and `opacity` to move or fade a card and everything on it as one. Its time span is a window: members show only inside it. `layers_ungroup` dissolves a group and keeps everything where it is on screen.
- **Auto layout** arranges a group's members like CSS flexbox, so they stay aligned when words change. Use it for buttons, chips, cards, lower thirds, lists and lyric lines: `layers_group {ids, name, layout: {flow: "row" | "column" | "wrap" | "grid", gap, padding, alignX, alignY}}`. The box hugs its members unless `width` or `height` is fixed, and is placed by its top-left corner (`layout.x`, `layout.y`; inferred from where the members sit when left out). `layout: {}` infers the flow and gap.
- **A rectangle behind the others becomes the container**: group a rect and its texts with a layout, and the rect's paints and corners move to the group, the padding is taken from it, and the container then hugs the text (a lower third or a button that fits any name).
- The front-most member comes first in the flow; with a layout, members are first sorted by where they sit along it. A member's `layoutItem` (`layer_patch`): `fillX`, `fillY` (stretch), `absolute` (keep its own place).
- `layout_set {id, layout: {...}}` changes it (fields merge; `padding` takes one number or `{top, right, bottom, left}`); `layout: null` removes it, keeping the members where they are. Under a layout, members' own `x` and `y` do not move them: animate the group, or (with the motion system) the members' entrances.
- `layer_create {groupId}` makes a layer in a group. `layers_list` shows each lane's `groupId`; a group's own lane holds only it and its members' lanes follow.

## Cutting and tidying time

- **Take time out:** `timeline_cut_ranges {id, ranges: [{startMs, endMs}], ripple}` razors the layer and removes the ranges (comp ms), one undo step. `ripple: "sync"` (default) keeps the comp in sync: on every lane at the layer's level, what is inside a cut goes, what spans it is shortened, what overlaps an edge is trimmed, and everything after slides back. `"lane"` closes only this layer's lane, `"none"` leaves holes (for B-roll). Cut between words or on a movement, never mid-word.
- **One cut by hand:** `timeline_split {id, atMs}`, `layers_delete`, then `timeline_close_gaps {ids}` (any layer on each lane to close). Locked layers stay and the rest close up to them.
- **Tidy:** after heavy restructuring, `timeline_compact {compId}` drops empty lanes and lets layers fall into free lanes behind, never changing what covers what. Locked, hidden and muted lanes stay put.
- Build scene after scene on few lanes: a new layer can share a lane with layers it never plays at the same time as (`trackId`).
- **The working range** (editor state: not saved, not undone): `session_set {workRange: {startMs, endMs}}` (at least 100 ms; `frameId` for another frame, default the open one) turns it on. Preview then loops inside it, `export_video` writes only that span (its reply gives `startMs` and `workRange`), and `view_frames` with no times spreads its tiles over it. `session_set {workRange: null}` turns it off and keeps the span. Use it to show or export one section; turn it off before exporting the whole video.

## Screen recordings

When the user asks to record their screen ("record my screen for 20 seconds", "record the browser window"), call `screen_record` with `durationMs` (ask how long when they did not say; they can stop early with the Stop button on screen). `source` is a display or window name ("Chrome", "Visual Studio Code"); omitted, the main display. Say before the call that the editor will step aside while it records. The editor minimises while a display records and comes back after; computer sound is on, the microphone and webcam (a picture-in-picture, bottom right) only when asked. The cursor is drawn by Windows, so it looks and moves exactly as it did. The result has `mediaId`, `layerId` and `cursorPath`: the cursor positions and clicks are kept beside the file (`<file>.cursor.json`, OpenScreen's layout) for zooms on clicks. Then cut it like any footage ("Raw footage: rough cuts").

**Polish a recording** (OpenScreen's look), in this order: cut it first (`media_remove_pauses` when it has speech), then `media_auto_zoom {id}` (zooms on the clicks: clicks close together share one zoom, the first 2.5 s and the last second stay wide; `depth` 1 to 6 forces one depth; the zooms are ordinary scale and position keys on the clip, so `keyframes_inspect` shows them and you can move them; it replaces the clip's scale and position keys, so run it again after a cut), then `screen_frame {id, padding?, radius?, shadow?, background?}` (the clip in a rounded card with a soft shadow over a gradient: `aurora`, `sunset`, `ocean`, `graphite`, `cream` or a colour; the zooms stay inside the card). A recording with no clicks gets no zooms: zoom by hand with scale and position keys where the action is. "Record my screen for 20 seconds and make it look nice" is `screen_record {durationMs: 20000, autoZoom: true}` then `screen_frame`.

## Raw footage: rough cuts

When the user brings their own footage (a talking head, a screen recording, B-roll) and asks to cut it, tighten it or make an edit of a length, read it first, then cut from the plan. The full recipe is 12 in `uve://skill/references/recipes.md`.

- **Read once:** `media_analyze {id}` on each clip (a layer id gives comp times). It finds the speech gate for that file, where the voice is, every word (Sarvam, verbatim, Indian languages or English; ₹0.49 a minute, free when heard before) and the shots, keeps them on the media and returns sentences and shots. Never re-run it without `force`; everything else reuses it.
- **Look at the picture:** `media_inspect {id}` is one tile per shot; `{id, shot}` or `{fromMs, toMs}` looks inside one. Write what each shot shows with `media_describe_shots {mediaId, shots: [{index, description}]}`, one plain line each (what is seen, any text on screen, the camera). If you cannot see images, `frames: 0` gives the words said in each shot instead; describe from those.
- **Tighten:** `media_remove_pauses {id, dryRun: true}` returns the plan in comp ms, a reason per cut (pause, filler "um", false start, retake) and flags for a person (a swallowed retake). `tightness`: `generous` (default; long pauses, ums, false starts, restarted sentences), `balanced` (also fillers such as "basically", "matlab", "accha" next to a pause), `tight` (short pauses, every filler phrase). Breathing room is built in: a pause after a sentence keeps 2.5× the air of one inside it. Show the plan when the user wants to review (`markers: true` puts it on the ruler), then apply with the same input and no `dryRun`: one undo step, captions and titles ripple with the words. `variant: true` cuts a copy of the frame and keeps the original.
- **Fit a length:** after tightening, if the edit is still too long, pick whole sentences to drop from `media_analyze`'s list and pass their spans to `timeline_cut_ranges` (one call, `ripple: "sync"`). Never cut inside a sentence to save time.
- **Find:** `media_search {query}` finds what is said (word for word or by sentence) and what shots show (their descriptions), with `inMs`/`outMs` of the sentence or shot for a select, and the layers that show it.
- **B-roll:** decide where each cutaway belongs (the sentence that talks about what it shows), then `timeline_place_broll {voiceId, cutaways: [{mediaId, startMs, endMs?, sourceStartMs?, relevance}]}`. Each is kept inside its sentence (0.15 s in from each end), at least 0.8 s, never over a `protected` beat (a punchline, the speaker's first line, a face reveal) or another cutaway; video is muted and scaled to cover. Give `relevance` honestly (0..1): under 0.35 is refused. Cover jump cuts with cutaways where you can; leave the speaker's face on the opening line and the closing line.
- **Then** captions (`media_transcribe {id, captions: true}` reuses the words), and the sound pass ("The loop", step 6).

## Exporting

- `export_video {compId?, path?, overwrite?, format?, ...}` renders exactly what `view_render` draws and waits until the file is written; the user sees the progress and can cancel. Its reply gives the path, size, frame rate, frames, codecs and length.
- `format` (AtomCut's ids): `mp4` (H.264 + AAC, the default), `mov` (lossless master: PNG frames and 16-bit PCM; large; for editing in Resolve, Premiere or After Effects), `webm` (VP9 + Opus), `gif` (loops forever, at most 50 fps), `png-seq` (a folder `<name>-png-seq` of `frame-000.png`…), `audio` (WAV, 48 kHz; only when the comp makes sound), and stills of one frame: `png`, `jpg`, `webp`.
- Options: `quality` (`draft`, `high` default, `master`: the video bitrate, or the JPG/WebP quality; WebP at `master` is lossless), `videoBitrate` (Mbps, overrides quality), `audioBitrate` (kbps, default 192), `scale` (0.05 to 4; 0.5 halves the size), `fps` (1 to 240; GIF is capped at 50), `includeAudio`, `motionBlur` (default on), `range` (`full` or `span`, the working range; default the working range when it is on).
- `transparent: true` leaves the comp's background out and keeps the alpha: `mov`, `webm`, `gif` (pixels under half alpha become clear), `png-seq`, `png`, `webp`. MP4, WAV and JPG cannot keep it and are refused. Use it for overlays, lower thirds and stickers meant to go over other footage.
- **Stills:** `format: "png"` (or `jpg`, `webp`) with `atMs` (comp ms; default the playhead) saves one frame, e.g. a thumbnail. A static frame always gives its still, and PNG is its default format. `atMs` with a moving format is refused.
- Refusals (a silent comp as audio, a static frame as a video, `transparent` on MP4, `range: "span"` with no working range) come back before any file is made, each with a hint.

## Frames and pages

A frame is a comp (AtomCut's name; `frameId` works wherever `compId` goes). Pages keep frames together: scenes on one, deliverables (master, reel, cutdowns, thumbnail) on another.

- `frame_create {name, width, height, fps, durationMs, background, mode, pageId}` returns `{id, pageId}`. `pageId` takes a page id or name; omitted, the frame goes on the page of the frame in view. `frame_update {id, ...}` changes the name, size (`scaleContents: true` scales the layers with the box; otherwise they stay put, centred), frame rate, duration, background, mode, page (`pageId` moves it), master `motionBlur`, or anything else by `ops` (`[{op: "set", path: "overflow", value: 0}]`). Without `id` it changes the frame open in the editor.
- `frame_duplicate {id, name?, pageId?}` makes an independent copy with new layer ids (read them with `layers_list`); what its nested layers own is copied with it. `frame_delete {id}` deletes a frame and its layers; the last frame stays, and a frame other layers show nested cannot go until they do.
- `page_create {name, frameIds?}` adds a page (names are unique) and can move frames onto it. `page_update {id, name?, index?}` renames or reorders it. `page_delete {id, moveTo?, comps?}`: a page that still holds frames needs `moveTo` (another page) or `comps: "delete"`; the last page stays.
- **Static frames** (`mode: "static"`) are one picture, not a timeline: a thumbnail, poster, social card or end card (1280×720, 1080×1350, 595×842 for A4). A static frame shows its still at any time asked, `atMs` (default 0 on create; on update, the playhead when the frame is open) picks which moment it holds, and every layer made in it spans the frame, so there is no timing to manage. Shown nested in another frame it holds still; it is silent. Switching mode with `frame_update {mode}` is lossless (static to timeline gives the animation back); `atMs` alone moves the still. `flipbook` frames are not in this build.
- Look at a static frame with `view_render` (any `atMs` gives the still; the result says `static: true`); `view_frames` returns one tile. `export_video` saves a static frame as a still (PNG by default, or `format: "jpg"` / `"webp"`); a video, GIF or sequence of it is refused.
- `overflow` (via `frame_create` or `frame_update` ops): where the frame is shown nested, `0` crops what spills past its box, `1` never crops; `null` (the default) lets each nested layer decide.

## Nested comps

- `layers_nest {ids, name}` precomposes layers into a comp of their own: one `nested` layer outside, their own timeline inside, nothing moves on screen. The comp hugs the layers and is transparent, so what moves past its box still shows (`fullFrame: true` makes it the parent's size and centre). Use it to animate a finished card or scene as one piece, to reuse a sequence, or to give a part its own speed or loop.
- `layer_create {kind: "comp", sourceCompId}` (AtomCut's `sourceFrameId` works too) shows any comp inside another, as large as that comp, for as long as it lasts unless `durationMs` says otherwise. It is a link: editing that comp changes every place it shows. `layers_makeUnique {ids}` gives a layer its own copy. `layers_list` shows each nested layer's `sourceCompId` and `inside` (`embedded` or `linked`); `doc_overview` marks embedded comps.
- Edit what is inside with the ordinary tools on the source comp's layers (`layers_list {compId: <sourceCompId>}`). A nested layer's own fields, by `layer_patch`: `playback {speed, loop: "none" | "loop" | "pingpong", loopLengthMs}`, `trimInMs` (the source time at the layer's start), `clipContent` (`null` auto: a comp with a visible background crops, a transparent one spills; `true` crops, `false` spills), `volume`, `muted`.
- **A play-once nested layer shows nothing past the end of its source**: trim it to the source's length, or loop it. Splitting or trimming the start of a looping one is refused (its loop starts at its start). Media inside plays at the nested layer's speed; a pingpong pass that runs back is silent.
- **A long piece in parts (one film, not joined files).** Anything over about 30 s, or built act by act, is one project: never separate projects joined afterwards (the bed restarts, loudness is matched per part, the joins are hard cuts).
  1. One frame per part (`frame_create {name: "Act 1 · Hook", width, height, durationMs}`), all the same size and frame rate. Each part holds its own pictures, captions, sound effects and its voice lines; no music inside a part.
  2. One master frame, as long as the film. Nest the parts one after another on one lane: `layer_create {compId: <master>, kind: "comp", sourceCompId: <part>, startMs, trackId}` (the same `trackId` for all, so the cuts are shared). Sounds stacks, clips and their categories inside the parts are heard in the master; the master's mix (leveler, ducking, loudness match) is the only one that counts.
  3. The bed goes on the master, under all the parts, one clip (or a few cross-faded) with one fade in and one fade out; the voice inside the parts ducks it.
  4. The joins: a transition on the incoming part's head, `transition_add {ids: [<part B>], edge: "in", preset}` (giving both ids also puts one on the first part's head and the last one's tail), or a match cut: end part A on the shape, colour or position part B begins with, and cut. A sound on the join (a whoosh peaking on the cut) goes on the master.
  5. A layer that carries on across a join (a logo, a mascot, a world the camera stays in) lives on the master over both parts, not copied into each.
  6. Check the joins with `view_frames` from 500 ms before to 500 ms after each, then export the master once: one `loudness` reply for the whole film.

## Styling part of a text

- `text_style {id, match: "count", style: {color: "#FFD60A", fontWeight: 900}}` styles a word (the first match; `occurrence: 2` or `"all"`), or `start` and `end` character indices (an emoji counts as one). `style` takes `fontFamily`, `fontSize`, `fontWeight`, `fauxBold`, `italic`, `decoration`, `textCase`, `color`, `letterSpacing`, `opacity` and `highlight` (`{color, radius, paddingX, paddingY}`: a pill behind those characters). It adds to what the characters already have; `clear: true` (or a list of fields) makes them plain again. A range's colour replaces the layer's fills there.
- `text_set {id, text}` changes the words and keeps each style on its words. A line is as tall as its largest font.
- The whole layer: `style.highlight` puts a pill behind each line (a caption chip, a kicker tag). `style.decoration` (`underline`, `strikethrough`) draws a line under or through the text, `style.textCase` (`upper`, `lower`, `title`) changes how it is shown while the stored text stays as typed (so word cues and styled ranges keep their places; use `upper` for kickers and labels instead of retyping), and `style.fauxBold` (0.02 to 0.04 reads as bold) thickens a family that has no heavier face.
- Keys on part of a text: `keyframes_set {id, property: "color" | "opacity" | "fontSize", range: {start, end}, keys}` (a word that flashes on the beat, a word that fades in last). The styled ranges' own paths are `range.<id>.color`, `.opacity`, `.fontSize` (`layer_properties` lists them). Emphasis is one idea per line.

## Text boxes

A text layer is sized one of three ways, like AtomCut:

- **Auto width** (the default, `maxWidth: null`): each paragraph is one line and the box hugs the widest.
- **Auto height** (`maxWidth` set): lines wrap between words at that width, and the box is as tall as the lines. Pass `maxWidth` to `layer_create` for captions, paragraphs and anything that must stay inside a column; never split lines by hand to make them fit.
- **Fixed** (`maxWidth` and `maxHeight`): the box keeps its size; `verticalAlign` (`top`, `middle`, `bottom`) places the lines in it, and `clipContent: true` hides what spills out.

`style.align` (`left`, `center`, `right`, `justify`) sets how lines sit inside the box; the pivot sets where the box sits. Keying `maxWidth` re-wraps the lines as it changes, which a scale cannot do. Set `maxWidth`, `maxHeight`, `verticalAlign` and `clipContent` back with `layer_patch` (`{"op": "set", "path": "maxWidth", "value": null}` returns to auto width). `layers_list` and `view_render` with `layers` show the real box.

## Motion, briefly

- `keyframes_set {id, path, keys: [{t, v, ease}]}` replaces the property's keys (pass `mode: "merge"` to add to them). Collect a property's keys and write them once. AtomCut's shape works too: `{id, property, keys: [{at, value, easing}], replace: false}`, and `frameId` wherever a `compId` goes.
- A key's `ease` shapes the move **leaving** it: `ease-out` to land an entrance, `ease-out-quart` or `ease-out-expo` for a snappier landing, `ease-in` to leave, `ease-in-out` between two rests, `ease-out-back` or `ease-out-elastic` for an overshoot, `ease-in-out-back`, `hold` for a cut, or a curve as `"cubic-bezier(x1, y1, x2, y2)"` or `{type: "bezier", x1, y1, x2, y2}`.
- Moves are written as poses: a key at each start, contact, extreme and rest, never one per frame. A rest is two equal keys; a cut is `hold` on the key before it. Something that moves through a middle point without stopping is either two keys (one continuous curve) or `keyframes_smooth` afterwards; a middle key on an eased move makes it stop there.
- Curved paths (`arc`, `in`, `out` on `position`): see "Motion paths" below.
- `keyframes_inspect {id}` shows what you keyed, in comp time; without `id` it flags baked tracks (a key on nearly every frame), which you should rewrite as a few poses.
- `keyframes_simplify {id}` repairs baked keys: it samples what plays on every frame and keeps the fewest keys (with fitted eases) that stay within `tolerance` (the property's own units; defaults 0.5 px for position and size, 0.25° for rotation, 0.3 % of the range otherwise). With `id` it takes every numeric track of that layer with 3 or more keys (or only `properties`); with `frameId` alone, every baked track of that comp. x and y are reduced together, so they stay one path. Colours, paths and switches are left alone. It keeps the timing, not the intent: for new motion, write a few poses instead. One undo step; the reply lists each track's keys before and after.
- Entrances take 300–700 ms, exits are quicker than entrances, and related elements are staggered by 40–80 ms. Keys are poses a person can edit, never one per frame.
- Text looks (103 templates: type, effects, entrance, typing sound): `catalog_list {"catalog": "text-templates", "query": "neon"}` finds one. `layer_create {kind: "text", template: "film-title", text: "…"}` makes a layer in that look; what you pass (`text`, `style`, `x`, `y`, `effects`) wins over the look. `template_apply {ids, templateId}` puts a look on text that exists, keeping its words, place, box, alignment and exit; `keepSize: true` keeps the font size. A caption look also sets how the spoken word looks on a layer with `caption_set` cues. A look is a strong starting point: then set the palette and type scale of the design system (`uve://skill/design-systems/default/DESIGN.md`).

## Motion paths

Key `position` (`[x, y]`) and x and y are written together as motion keys: one per pose. `arc`, `in` and `out` work in either key shape (`{at, value}` or `{t, v}`). A segment between two motion keys is a straight line unless a handle or an arc curves it. The user sees the path in the viewport when the layer is selected and can drag its points and handles.

- **Hops, throws, bounces: `arc`.** `arc: h` on a key turns the segment leaving it into a thrown arc that peaks `h` px above the higher of its two ends (`h < 0` sags `|h|` below the lower end: a swing, a dip). The key's easing then defaults to `linear`, which is real gravity: steady sideways speed, a true parabola. Keep it linear; an ease on an arc makes the throw hang or rush. The peak falls at mid-time only when both ends are level. One key per contact; a rest is the same point keyed again, and the take-off key carries the next arc:

  ```json
  {"id": "Ball", "property": "position", "keys": [
    {"at": 0, "value": [-500, 300], "arc": 320},
    {"at": 600, "value": [-120, 300]},
    {"at": 700, "value": [-120, 300], "arc": 160},
    {"at": 1100, "value": [140, 300], "arc": 70},
    {"at": 1350, "value": [300, 300]}
  ]}
  ```

  Bounces lose height and time together: each arc about half the last, each flight about 70 % as long. An arc needs a later position key; an arc and the next key's `in` handle on one segment are refused ("One curve per segment"); an arc beats an `out` on the same key.
- **Any other curve: handles.** `out: [dx, dy]` on a key leans the segment leaving it, `in: [dx, dy]` leans the segment arriving, both in px relative to that key; `null` clears one. A curved segment's missing handle is a third of the way along the chord. Easing still times the move: the eased time runs along the curve, so the speed changes with it.
- **Tracing a drawn line** (a dot riding a path layer, a pen tip, a plane on a route): the motion path is the drawing. Key `position` at each anchor point of the path, with each cubic `C c1 c2 p` as the `out` of the key before (`c1 − start`) and the `in` of the key after (`c2 − p`). A path layer is centred on its outline's box, so add `layer.x − boxCentreX` (and the same for y) to the points of `d`. Use `linear` and give each segment time in proportion to its length for an even pen speed.
- **The layer does not turn with the path.** Key `rotation` yourself where the direction changes (or leave it upright).
- **Read it back:** `keyframes_inspect {id}` prints `↷(out dx,dy in dx,dy)` after the x value of every curved key, e.g. `0 -500 ↷(out 126.7,-426.7) linear · 600 -120 ↷(in -126.7,-426.7) ease-in-out · 700 -120 ↷(out 86.7,-213.3) linear · …`. The handles paste back as `out` and `in` (with `clock: "comp"` for the printed times); its `curvedKeys` counts them. `keyframes_simplify` leaves curved paths alone.
- **Edit one point: `motion_edit {id, atMs, op}`.** `atMs` is **comp time**, as `keyframes_inspect` prints it (layer time is `atMs` minus the layer start), and must sit on a motion key except for `insert` and `bend`. Ops: `move {value: [x, y]}` (handles travel with the point), `insert` (a new point on the path at that time; shape and timing unchanged), `delete` (the point and its handles), `smooth` (handles that flow through the neighbours; an end point gets one), `corner` (no handles: a sharp turn), `bend {u, point: [x, y]}` (the segment at `atMs` passes through `point` at `u`, 0…1 exclusive, of the way along), `handle {side: "in" | "out", value: [dx, dy] | null, mirror?}` (`mirror: true` sets the other side opposite, so the point stays smooth). A wrong time is refused with the motion keys' times in the hint.
- **Flow through the keys: `keyframes_smooth {id, properties?, fromMs?, toMs?}`.** It rewrites the easing so the value keeps moving through each key instead of easing to a stop (the timeline's Smooth button). On `position` with three keys or more it also gives the path round handles, and keeps the speed along the curve the same either side of each key. Use it for a flight through waypoints, a camera drifting past several marks, a slow orbit. It **replaces the easing** (and position handles) of every key in range: run it before hand-tuning, never after an `arc` hop (the hop's gravity and its rest stops would be smoothed away; give it a `fromMs`/`toMs` that leaves the hops out). `fromMs`, `toMs` are comp time and inclusive; without `properties` it smooths every numeric track of the layer. Two keys stay a straight line.
- Check a path with `view_frames` at the take-off, the apex and the landing (not on even spacing); motion blur follows the curve.

## Entrances and exits (animation presets)

`animation_set {id, slot: "in" | "out", preset}` is the fastest way to animate anything; reach for it before keyframes.

- **Text per unit** (target `"text"`, the default on text layers): each letter, word or line (between line breaks) animates on its own, unit i starting `staggerMs` after unit i − 1. Knobs: `unit`, `durationMs` (each unit's own), `staggerMs`, `delayMs` (after the layer's start; for `out`, before its end), `easing`, `jitterMs` (a human typing rhythm), `scatterPx`, `scatterDeg`, and the preset's `params`. Never make one layer per word or letter to stagger them.
- **The whole layer** (target `"layer"`, any visual layer): it plays from the layer's start (`in`) or ends at its end (`out`). It has no delay: start the layer where the entrance should begin. A text layer has both kinds of slot, so a title can `pop-in` as a whole while its words `fade-up`.
- `catalog_list {"catalog": "animations"}` lists the 44 presets (tags give each one's scope and default unit); `catalog_get` gives the defaults and knobs with ranges. Families: fade (fade, fade-up, fade-down, blur-in, soft-focus, unblur-up, fade-through), slide (slide-up/down/left/right, fly-in, drift, nudge, slide-stagger, slide-through), scale (pop-in, scale-in, zoom-out, punch, grow, breathe), bounce (bounce-in, drop, rubber, settle), spin (flip-up/down, spin-in, roll-in, swing, pendulum), type (typewriter, wave, cascade, ripple, trace), stylize (whip, glitch, strobe, smear, shutter, flicker-on) and `draw` (strokes draw on: shape and path layers, piece by piece; a layer without a stroke is inked in its fill colour).
- Pairings that work: `unblur-up` or `fade-up` per line or word for titles, `pop-in` for badges and stickers, `typewriter` per letter for terminals and UI, `slide-stagger` for lists, `draw` for line art and logos, `whip` or `punch` for energetic beats.
- An exit without a pose of its own plays the entrance backwards. Swapping presets keeps the slot's timing (and the knobs the presets share): pass `durationMs` and `staggerMs` again when the new preset needs different ones. `preset: null` removes a slot. The result gives `compMs` (when it runs, in comp time), `units`, and `warnings` when it outlasts the layer.
- Blur in a preset (blur-in, unblur-up, whip, punch, smear, soft-focus) is real Gaussian blur, in the layer's pixels.

## Effects

`effects_add {id, type, values}` adds a card to a layer's stack and returns `effectId`; the first card applies first. Layer effects work on the layer's drawn pixels, the same on text, shapes, paths, images, video, groups and nested comps (put one card on a group rather than the same card on each member). Text effects (`domain: "text"`) are drawn under each glyph and move with its animation.

- **Layer effects:** `shadow` (drop shadow), `innerShadow`, `glow` (auto: the layer's own colours; `solid` with `color`; `inner`), `longShadow`, `blur`, `directionalBlur`, `zoomBlur`, `spinBlur`, `sharpen`, `adjust` (brightness, contrast, saturate, hueDeg, grayscale, sepia), `vignette`, `stroke` (an outline of the silhouette: outside, center, inside), `rgbSplit`, `shine` (a band of light on its own clock: `duration` s per pass, `delay`, `loop`; `duration: 0` and key `position` to drive it by hand), and the analog cards `grain`, `noise` (a procedural field laid over the layer, or `displace` to warp it), `scanlines` (line, dot, slot or grille masks, roll, flicker, tube curvature) and `filmDamage` (dust, scratches, hairs, flicker, gate weave); the distortions `bulge`, `wave` (a travelling wave: flags, jelly, heat), `twirl`, `lensDistort`, `goo` (put it on a GROUP of shapes near each other: they fuse), `roughenEdges` (torn, chalk, melt, rust; only the outline moves), `boil` (hand-drawn line boil: `variants` drawings looped at `rate`, 8–12 reads hand-made) and `glitch` (short bursts on cuts, 100–300 ms); colour and stylize: `colorGrade` (temperature, tint, exposure, contrast, saturation, vibrance, hue, lift, gamma and gain per channel), `gradientMap` (duotone or three colours), `posterize`, `overlay` (Fill & gradient: paint a colour or ramp inside the layer's shape), `chromaKey` (green screen: `color`, `tolerance`, `softness`, `spill`, `choke`, `feather`), `halftone` (mono, duotone, color or cmyk screens), `pixelate` (mosaic, or `shape: "dot"` for an LED sign) and `bevel` (inner, outer, pillow, emboss); light and material: `lightRays` (shafts from the layer's bright parts), `lensFlare`, `lightning` (bolt between two box points, burst, along the outline or inside the shape) and `glass` (liquid glass: it bends and frosts what is behind the layer, so it needs a busy backdrop; a pill or panel, one per shot).
- **Text effects:** `shadow`, `glow` (in the glyph's colour by default), `extrude` (solid depth), `echo` (fading copies). `source: "text"` takes each glyph's colour; `"custom"` uses `color`.
- Offsets and sizes are layer px and turn and scale with the layer. Colours take `#rrggbbaa`: a shadow's strength is its colour's alpha.
- Number and colour fields animate at `lfx.<effectId>.<field>` (`tfx.` for text effects) through `keyframes_set` or `layer_set`; `catalog_get layer-effects <type>` lists the fields with ranges and defaults. `effects_update {id, effectId, values | enabled | index | remove}` changes a card; a keyed field is set with `layer_set` and `atMs` instead.
- `layer_create` takes `effects: [{type, ...fields}]` and `style.textEffects` the same way, so a text look's `layerCreate` from `catalog_get text-templates` arrives with its glow, extrude or outline.
- Taste: one hero effect per shot, at most two on text. Shadows: large blur, low alpha, offset down. Glow reads on dark grounds with a saturated colour. Animated effects (a shine, a blur pull, an RGB split burst) are brief, 200–600 ms, tied to a beat or a reveal.
- Analog cards re-roll on a seeded clock (`rate` looks a second, `jitter`, `stutter`, `seed` picks the take) only with `animate: true`; grain and noise arrive still, scanlines and film damage moving. Keep grain still and low (0.05–0.15) under type; turn `animate` on for a film look over footage. Leave film damage `splices` off unless the reel is meant to be broken.
- Not in this build yet: the colour node graph (`grade`), saved effect looks, audio effects; `effects_add` says which step brings each.

## Transitions

Mostly hard cuts. `transition_add {ids, preset, edge: "in" | "out" | "any", durationMs, easing, params, color}` puts a transition on a cut for a reason: `cross-dissolve` for time passing, `dip-to-color` or `fade` for section breaks, `whip-pan` or `push` for energy between similar shots, `zoom`, `glitch-cut` or `shake-cut` for social beats. 200–500 ms, one style per piece.

- Between two layers that touch on one lane it is centred on the cut: the outgoing layer holds its last frame past its end, the incoming one its first frame before its start. On a lone layer's head it plays from its start (it arrives); on its tail up to its end (it leaves). Layers must abut exactly on the same lane to share a cut (`timeline_close_gaps` closes gaps).
- 23 presets (`catalog_list transitions`; `catalog_get` for knobs): dissolves (cross-dissolve, fade, dip-to-color, blur-dissolve, additive-dissolve, soft-dissolve), wipes (linear-wipe, iris, iris-close, clock-wipe, wedge-wipe, barn-door), motion (push, slide, slide-apart, whip-pan, zoom, roll), stylize (pop, spin, dip-to-white "Flash", glitch-cut, shake-cut). Direction knobs: `angle` 180 brings the next shot in from the left.
- Sound crosses over with it (equal power; a dip for dip-to-color). `layers_list` lists each lane's transitions; `transition_remove {transitionId}`. Adding on a cut that has one replaces it; a too-long duration is shortened to fit, with a warning.

## Captions

A caption is a text layer whose words light up as they are spoken: each cue (a span of the text with its times) is unread, in focus, then read, and each state has a look. `caption_set {id, words: [{text?, startMs, endMs}]}` matches the words in order (text omitted takes the next word); or `cues` by character index; or `even: {unit: "word", fromMs, toMs}` to spread them. Times are layer time unless `timesIn: "comp"`.

- `states`: `unread`, `focus`, `read`, each `{style: {color, opacity, fontWeight, highlight}, scale}` (merged; AtomCut's `{overlay: {props}}` works too). Defaults: unread 45 % opacity; focus dark ink on an amber pill at 108 %; read full. `motion: {durationMs, easing, travel}`: `travel: true` makes one pill glide from word to word (karaoke).
- Lyric styles: synced lyrics (read 0.35 opacity, upcoming 0.55, the line bold), karaoke (focus colour and a gliding pill), kinetic one-word (one word per layer, big, `pop-in` per cue with a sound).
- A voice take knows its words (`generate_speech`, `media_transcribe`): `media_transcribe {id: audioLayerId, captions: true}` makes the captions, one per phrase; `{captions: {id}}` re-times an existing one. Without a voice, time the words from the script or the beat. `remove: true` makes it plain text.

## Voice and Indian languages

Voices come from Sarvam (Bulbul): Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Gujarati, Punjabi, Odia and Indian English, every voice in every language. It costs the user money (about ₹3 per 1,000 characters; listening ₹0.49 a minute; translating ₹2 per 1,000 characters), so write the script first and generate once. The same text, voice and settings again reuse the file and cost nothing. Read `uve://skill/references/voice.md` before any voice work.

- **One take per phrase or scene**, never one long take cut up: `generate_speech {lines: [...], place: {startMs, compId}, gapMs}` makes one take per line, placed so each voice starts where the last ended plus `gapMs`. The timeline then takes its timing from each take's real length; build the scene's visuals to the take, not the take to the scene.
- **Words are timed for you.** Each take keeps its script and word times (`media.speech`): `media_transcribe {id, captions: true}` turns a placed take into a word-synced caption; the words also tell you when to land a beat (a pop on the key word, a cut at a phrase end).
- **Another language:** `comp_localize {compId, language, keep: ["BrandName"]}` makes a copy of the comp that speaks it: text translated, each take spoken again, a take that runs long read up to 1.25× faster and then given room (everything after waits), captions re-timed. Run it once per language from the original. `text_translate` alone translates text layers.
- **Scripts render themselves:** text in Devanagari, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Gujarati, Gurmukhi or Odia falls back to the Noto family of its script, so any Latin display face keeps its Latin letters and the rest draws correctly. To set the whole line in one face, pick a Google family that has the script (`fonts_list {script: "devanagari"}` (or tamil, bengali…): Mukta, Hind, Baloo 2, Tiro Devanagari Hindi).
- **In the speaker's own voice** ("in my voice", a dub of the user's own talking head): Sarvam's instant voice cloning, only for the user's own voice or one they have the right to use. `comp_localize {language, ownVoice: true}` re-speaks each audio take in its own speaker's voice. For a talking-head video: `media_analyze` it, `text_translate` each sentence, `generate_speech {lines, language, voice: "clone", reference: <the video layer>, place: {startMs}}` one line per sentence at that sentence's time, then mute the video (`layer_patch {id, ops: [{op: "set", path: "muted", value: true}]}`) and caption the new takes (`media_transcribe {id, captions: true}`). A cloned line is at most 1,000 characters.
- **Newest voices:** `model: "bulbul:v4-flash"` (more natural, cheaper); its speakers are named name_language_style (`voices_list {language}` gives `v4Voices`); without one, a narrator of the language is picked.
- `sarvam_usage` shows what this editor has spent; a failed call says when the key is missing or credits are used up: stop and tell the user.

## Sounds on motion (the sounds stack)

Any layer fires sounds from its own motion, re-synced whenever the timing changes. A layer's `sounds` is a list of voices, set with `layer_patch` (`insert` into `sounds[-]`; ids are minted) or `layer_create {sounds: [...]}`:

```json
{ "pack": "pop", "volume": 0.32, "pitch": 1, "randomness": 0.6, "phase": "in", "trigger": { "kind": "text" }, "anchor": "start", "offsetMs": 0 }
```

- `trigger.kind`: `text` fires on every unit (letter, word or line) of the text entrance (`phase: "in"`) or exit; `layer` on the whole-layer entrance or exit; `transition` on the transition at its head (in) or tail (out). `anchor`: the start, middle (`peak`) or end of each motion.
- `pack`: synthesised `pop` (bubbly, the best general choice), `mechanical` and `thock` (keyboards), `soft`, `vintage` (typewriter-like), `digital`; or `keys`, recorded keypresses (a real keyboard, a different take per hit). Each pack keeps its own level, as AtomCut's do: `mechanical` and `digital` are quiet ticks (about 10 dB under), `thock` and `pop` loud, so leave their volumes near the default rather than evening them out. Or `custom: {assetId, hitMs}` with a media asset with sound (`hitMs`: where the file's hit is).
- `randomness` (0.6 by default) varies each hit's volume (down by up to 65 % × randomness) and pitch (±17.5 % × randomness); hits always land on their moment.
- More sounds: `sounds_list {query}` searches the packs, this project's sounds and the CC0 sound library (about 4,500 sounds). Search by what you hear, in one or two words (`whoosh`, `soft click`, `paper`, `bell`): every word must match, so a long phrase finds nothing. `#tag` requires a tag (`whoosh #cinematic`). Results carry `hitMs`, `durationMs` and, for about 100 common picks, `use` (what the sound suits and how it lands): read it before you pick. `sounds_use {id}` fetches a library sound, adds it to the project with its credit, measures its hit and returns the exact `{pack}` or `{custom}` value to put in a voice; use that value as it is.
- Placement: put a sound on the layer that causes it (its own `sounds`, or a clip inside the same group or nested comp), never a pile of loose sound lanes at the root. `hitMs` is the file's transient and lands on the moment, so a custom sound hits on the frame of impact. A sound lands at its anchor plus `offsetMs`, with the file's `hitMs` on that moment, and `peak` is the middle of the motion, not the sound's peak. So: a whoosh on a layer entrance uses `trigger: {kind: "layer"}` and `anchor: "end"` (it arrives as the entrance finishes) or `"start"`; a whoosh on a transition uses `trigger: {kind: "transition"}` and `anchor: "peak"` (the middle of the transition window is the cut); an impact uses `anchor: "end"` of the motion that causes it; a riser has its `hitMs` at its end, so use `anchor: "end"` of the motion that is the downbeat, or place it as a clip that ends on the downbeat. A voice fires only when its motion exists: `trigger: text` needs a text animation (`animation_set`), `layer` needs an entrance or exit on that layer, `transition` needs a transition on that edge (`transition_add`). With no motion it makes no sound and no error. For a beat with no motion, place a short audio clip on the beat (a media layer in the same group, tagged `sfx`).
- **Choosing.** Before placing sounds, read `uve://skill/references/sound.md`: a palette of 4–6 sounds from one family for the whole piece, a sound per kind of motion (appear, slide in, land, drip, count, type, stamp, build, reveal), and what to avoid. Results carry `quality` (`motion`, `foley`, `game`) and `use`: in a polished piece pick `motion` or `foley` with a `use` that fits; a name alone can mislead. One-shot effects, as voices or as clips, sit at volume 0.2–0.35 (the scene's one big hit up to 0.5); a recorded sound keeps `pitch` within 0.85–1.15; never the same file over and over (randomness, or 2–3 takes).
- Sounds fire from inside nested comps and worlds too, in the outer comp's time, with the nested layer's volume, mute and `soundBus`: a part built in its own comp keeps its sounds when nested in a master comp.
- Taste: per word or per element, rarely per letter (letters only for typewriters, `mechanical` or `thock` at 0.15–0.25). One-shot UI sounds sit at volume 0.15–0.35 under the music; a stream of hits (typing) at 0.15–0.3 with `randomness` 0.5–0.6, never above 0.5. `mechanical` is the soft default for typing, `thock` for a harder keyboard. Lower `pitch` for heavy elements, higher for small ones. One sound family per piece; silence is a tool. Text looks with a sound bring it.

## The mix (categories, leveler, loudness)

Every sounding layer goes to a category (bus); the comp's mix then levels each category and the whole. The defaults are right for most pieces: balance with the categories, not with clip volumes.

- **Tag every media clip.** `layer_create` tags audio and video from the file name (voice, narration, vo, music, song, bgm, ambience, sfx, whoosh, click…); a name that matches nothing goes to master, unrouted: levelled to −18 LUFS instead of its bus target, never ducked, and muted whenever any category is soloed. The guess is a substring match on the file name (`white-noise.wav` has `hit` in it and becomes `sfx`, `training.mp3` has `rain` in it and becomes `ambience`), so confirm every tag and fix the wrong ones. For a file you place yourself, pass `soundBus: "music"` to `layer_create` and skip the patch. `layer_get` shows a clip's `soundBus` (no `soundBus` means unrouted), and `sound_category` with no action counts each category's layers. Set the rest: `layer_patch {id, ops: [{op: "set", path: "soundBus", value: "music"}]}`. Fired sounds (the sounds stack) go to `sfx` when untagged. A nested layer passes its `soundBus` to the layers inside that have none.
- **Categories** (`sound_category`, no action lists them): `voice` (Dialogue, target −16 LUFS), `music` (−20), `sfx` (Effects, −19, measured on its loudest moment), `ambience` (−28). The leveler measures each sound and trims it toward its bus target (`soundMix.leveler`, on, strength 0.8, loud sounds turned down); rapid-fire hits sit 7 dB under one-shots, and speech gets a gentle compressor. Leave it on. To make a bus louder or quieter, move its fader: `sound_category {action: "update", id: "music", volume: 0.8}`.
- **Ducking is a rule, not volume keys.** Music ducks 10 dB under the voice bus by default (150 ms in, starting early so the first word is already clear, 500 ms out). Change it with `sound_category {action: "update", id: "music", ducking: [{under: "voice", depthDb: 14}]}` (the list replaces the rules).
- **The bed.** A piece longer than about 3 s has a music file in the project, cut to the comp (`durationMs` on `layer_create`; after `media_import`, `timeline_trim` to the comp end), with a fade of 1–3 s in and out as `volume` keys, for a 10 s comp: `keyframes_set {id, path: "volume", keys: [{t: 0, v: 0}, {t: 2000, v: 1}, {t: 8000, v: 1}, {t: 10000, v: 0}]}` (`t` is relative to the layer start, so the fade-out key sits at `durationMs` minus the fade). Media has no loop: a bed shorter than the comp just ends, so repeat it as a second clip or report it. The file comes from the user or the project: one in `media_list` goes on with `layer_create {kind: "media", mediaId, soundBus: "music", startMs: 0, durationMs}`, a file on disk with `media_import {localPath, place}`; the CC0 library has no beds, so never pass a `sounds_list` result off as one. With no music file, skip the bed, say so in the report and `report_issue` it. Pops alone are too sparse to carry the loudness.
- **Voiceover.** One clip per line, placed whole, never cut inside a word. The voice is the loudest thing in the piece. Ducking only works while someone speaks: between lines the bed comes back up, so in a narrated piece pull the music fader down too (`sound_category {action: "update", id: "music", volume: 0.6}`, about −4.5 dB), which leaves the bed about 9 to 10 dB under the voice in the pauses as well, the way a finished launch film sits. A dense, loud track (drums, synths) may need 0.5.
- **Loudness match.** On by default for new projects (`soundMix.loudness`, target −14 LUFS): the export measures the whole mix and trims it to the target, up or down, never taking more than 3 dB off the loudest peak (more flattens the hits of effects). A project opened from an older file has it off: check `soundMix.loudness.enabled` and turn it on with `frame_update {soundMix: {loudness: {enabled: true}}}`. Change the target with `frame_update {soundMix: {loudness: {targetLufs: -16}}}`; master fader, leveler and limiter (ceiling −1 dB) are in the same `soundMix`. After `export_video`, `loudness` in its reply gives `integratedLufs`, `truePeakDbtp` and `matchDb`. `loudness` is only in the reply for formats with sound (not `gif`, `png-seq` or stills). Integrated −16 to −14 LUFS with `truePeakDbtp` at or below −1 is right. If it is far off, find the cause: no bed, an untagged clip, a muted or soloed category, or the match switched off. A sparse mix with big peaks over a quiet body stops short of the target, because the peak cap limits how much the match can raise it: add a bed or lower the peaks, do not raise gains.
- On a hard cut inside a sustained sound, a 20–50 ms `volume` fade stops a click (every layer's edges already get 2 ms, except at seamless splits).

## Motion blur

On by default, like a film camera: every layer that moves while the shutter is open (a 180° shutter, half a frame) is blurred along its motion, in previews, renders and exports. A still layer costs nothing.

- The comp's master: `frame_update {id, motionBlur: {enabled, preset, shutterAngle, shutterPhase, weighting, sampling, combine, strength, amount, maxSamples}}`. Presets: `film` (180°), `crisp` (90°, snappier), `long` (light added, a full frame), `comet` (a tail behind), `anticipate` (a smear ahead), `whip` (exaggerated, for whip pans), `ghost` (discrete echoes).
- A layer: `layer_patch` `motionBlur.enabled` (false keeps type crisp while it is read), or `motionBlur.override` (its own shutter, any of the fields above). Its shutter keys at `mblur.shutterAngle`, `mblur.shutterPhase`, `mblur.strength`, `mblur.amount`.
- `sampling`: `transform` (default: the layer placed at each moment, exact for arcs and spins), `velocity` (one straight smear, cheapest), `content` (drawn anew at each moment, so animation inside it blurs too; costly).
- Turn it off for type that must stay crisp while it moves slowly under reading; leave it on for whips, drops and big slides. Check with `view_frames` across the fast part.

## 3D worlds

A world is a comp that has `world`: a stage with real depth, seen through cameras and lit by lights. Your flat layers stand in it as pictures. Make one when the idea needs a camera move, parallax or depth. For a push-in or a drift on a flat comp, a `scale` key is cheaper.

**Space.** Z is up, X is right, Y points away from the front view, and 1 unit is 1 px. Positions are `x`, `y`, `z` in these axes: to raise a thing, raise `z`; to push it back, raise `y`. Turns are `rotationX`, `rotationY`, `rotation` (about Z), in degrees, standard right-handed turns, applied Z first, then Y, then X. A 2D layer in a world is a flat picture standing in the XZ plane, facing the default camera. A picture reads its scales as `scaleX` (width), `scaleY` (height) and `scaleZ` (depth). `world_inspect` and `layers_list {compId: <world id>}` show everything in world numbers.

**The default camera.** With no camera clip, the world is seen from [0, −D, 0] looking along +Y (D is about 2667 px at 1920 wide). It shows the plane `y = 0` exactly as the flat comp did, so adding depth changes nothing until a camera or a move does.

- **`world_create {name?, width?, height?, fps?, durationMs?, starter?, transparent?, pageId?}`** makes a world frame (defaults 1920×1080, 30 fps, 10 s, dark grey background; `width` and `height` 16 to 8192, `fps` 1 to 240, `pageId` a page id or name; `transparent: true` clears it, for nesting over flat layers). `starter` (default true) adds a camera at [736, −693, 496] aimed at the origin, a 1500 W point light at [408, 100, 590], a "Studio" environment at 0.3 and a 200 px grey cube at the origin, each spanning the frame. Pass `starter: false` for an empty stage with the default camera. Returns `frameId`.
- **`world_move_into {ids | compId, name?}`** is the usual way in: it takes flat layers (or `compId`, a whole frame) and puts them in a new world, nested full frame, so the picture is the same and now has depth. It returns `nestedId` (the layer in the flat comp), `frameId` (the world), and `approximate` (layer ids) when a layer's keyed turns could not be converted exactly. Positions and keys are converted for you: flat `y` becomes world `z` (negated), flat `z` becomes world `y`, and the key tracks are renamed the same way; curved motion paths come out straight. Audio, adjustments and 3D objects stay behind. Edit the layers inside with the ordinary tools on the world's layers. It refuses a frame that is already a world.
- **`world_add {kind, position?, rotation?, lookAt?, ...}`** adds `mesh` (see Meshes), `camera`, `light`, `controller` (an empty point that nothing draws: aim at it or move it) or `text` (`head` is refused: faces are not in this build). `position` is [x, y, z] (default [0, 0, 0]); `rotation` is [x, y, z] degrees; `lookAt` is a point [x, y, z] or a layer id (aimed once, at the new object's start time) and gives way to `rotation`. A camera with neither looks along +Y. Other fields: `light` (the type, see Lights), `intensity` (a light's strength, at least 0), `color` (`#rrggbb` or `#rrggbbaa`: a light's colour, the text colour or a mesh's colour), `focalLength` (mm, 1 to 5000, default 50; smaller is wider), `scale` (one number or [x, y, z]), `text`, `name`, `startMs`, `durationMs` (default the whole frame), and for meshes `primitive`, `params`, `assetId`, `material`. `primitive`, `params`, `assetId` and `material` are refused on other kinds. `catalog_list lights` describes each light.
- **Lights.** `light` is one of `point` (a bulb), `sun` (parallel light along its −Z, the key light), `spot` (a cone down its −Z), `area` (a soft panel facing its −Z), `environment` (the surroundings, lit from all sides and in reflections; only the first counts) and `hemisphere` (ambient fill). Default `intensity`: point 1000, spot 1500, area 800 (watts); sun 3, environment 1, hemisphere 0.6 (strengths). Point, spot and sun cast shadows (when `world_look` has `shadows` on); area, environment and hemisphere do not. The other light fields are set with `layer_patch` under `light.`: `range` (point and spot: px where the light ends, 0 is endless), `spotAngle` (1 to 180°, default 45), `spotBlend` (0 to 1, default 0.15), `width` and `height` (area, px, default 100), `groundColor` (hemisphere: the colour from below; `color` is from above), `castShadow` (default true), `shadowSoftness` (0 to 20, default 2), `environment` (`studio` default, `soft`, `sunset`, `dawn`, `night`, `overcast`) and `showBackground` (draw the surroundings behind the scene, default false). `light.intensity` and `light.color` key.
- **Cameras and cuts.** The live camera at a moment is the topmost visible camera clip that covers it (front lane first; hidden lanes and disabled layers are skipped). With none, the default camera. A cut is two camera clips side by side in time, or one on a front lane over another: trim and place them with `timeline_move` and `timeline_trim`. A new camera is on a new front lane, so it is live at once. `world_camera {activate: <cameraId>, atMs?}` makes that camera live (it must be on the timeline at `atMs`); `world_camera {fromDefault: true}` adds a camera at the default pose behind the others, a starting point to move; with neither it reports `active` (the live camera's id, or `"default"`) at `atMs`.
- **Keyable fields.** A camera's `x`, `y`, `z`, `rotationX`, `rotationY`, `rotation`, `camera.focalLength` (a zoom without moving), `camera.dof.focusDistance`, `camera.dof.fStop`; a light's `light.intensity`, `light.color` and position; any picture's `x`, `y`, `z`, `scaleX`, `scaleY`, `scaleZ`, `opacity`. Use `keyframes_set` or `layer_set`. `world_add`'s `lookAt` aims once; to keep aiming while things move, use `world_track` (below). For a camera on the level (no `rotationY`), the turns that aim at a point offset by (dx, dy, dz) are `rotation = atan2(−dx, dy)` and `rotationX = 90 + atan2(dz, hypot(dx, dy))`, in degrees. Depth of field is drawn: see `world_look`.
- **`world_track {id, lookAt?, follow?, followRotation?, childOf?, atMs?}`** ties a layer to another in the same world, from then on (camera, light, mesh, picture, controller or group; `null` removes that tie, and giving one again replaces it):
  - `lookAt: <id>` keeps it turned to face the target while either moves (a camera or light aims its view, a picture its face); its own turns are ignored.
  - `follow: <id>` makes it ride along: its own position becomes its OFFSET from the target, so set the distance it keeps as its position (`[0, -900, 250]` rides 900 px in front and 250 above). `followRotation: true` also turns it with the target (the offset turns too).
  - `childOf: <id>` parents it: it stays exactly where it is at `atMs` (default the playhead when the frame is open, else the layer's start) and from then on moves, turns and scales with the parent. Chain them for a rig (moon → planet → controller). Loops are refused.
  - A tie on a group carries its members. Ties are worked out in the order child of, follow, look at, so a camera can be a child of a controller and still look at a hero. `world_inspect` shows each object where its ties put it, with its `constraints`.
  - `influence` (0 to 1, default 1) blends a tie with where the layer would be without it, and `enabled` switches it: `layer_patch {id, ops: [{op: "set", path: "constraints[id=<cns id>].influence", value: 0.5}]}`. IK (`ik`) is not in this build yet: key the joints' rotation.
- **`world_material {ids, preset?, values?}`** makes a picture take light. A picture with no material is unlit: exactly the flat picture, ignoring every light. Presets: `plastic`, `matte`, `clay`, `ceramic`, `rubber`, `metal`, `chrome`, `gold`, `copper`, `carpaint`, `glass`, `frosted`, `velvet`, `pearl`, `neon`, `toon`, `flat` (`catalog_list materials` says what each is for). A preset replaces the old material; `values` then sets fields on top (`model` pbr, toon or unlit; `color` tint, `null` leaves the picture's own; `emission` the colour it glows with; `roughness`, `metalness`, `transmission`, `clearcoat`, `clearcoatRoughness`, `sheen`, `iridescence` all 0 to 1; `emissionStrength` 0 to 100; `ior` 1 to 2.333; `thickness` in px; `toonSteps` 2 to 16; `doubleSided`; `source` `file` or `custom`, see Models; unknown fields are refused); values alone change the material as it is. `preset: null` removes it. `roughness`, `metalness`, `emission`, `emissionStrength` and `transmission` key. Pictures and meshes take one: not cameras, lights or controllers. Metal and glass need an `environment` light to have anything to reflect (starter worlds have one).
- **`world_look {compId?, toneMapping?, exposure?, shadows?, shadowQuality?, mist?, bloom?, ao?, floor?, cameraId?, dof?}`** sets the world's finish. Only the fields you give change, and giving `bloom`, `ao` or `dof` fields turns that effect on unless you pass `enabled: false` (`true` or `false` alone switches it).
  - `toneMapping` (`agx` default, `aces`, `neutral`, `none`), `exposure` in stops (−10 to 10, default 0; each stop doubles the light), `shadows` on or off (every light's), `shadowQuality` (`low` 512, `medium` 1024, `high` 2048 px shadow map), `mist` (`{enabled, color, start, end}` in px from the camera: fog that fades far things).
  - `bloom {strength 0.8 (0–3), radius 0.5 (0–1), threshold 0.75 (0–1), source}`: what is brighter than the threshold glows into its surroundings. `source: "bright"` (default) glows anything bright, a white wall included; `source: "emission"` glows only what a material emits (`material.emission` with `emissionStrength` 2–6: neon, screens, a lit logo), the right choice in a lit scene. Taste: strength 0.4–1.2, radius 0.3–0.7.
  - `ao {radius 60 px (1–5000), strength 1 (0–2)}`: contact shadows where things meet (a product on a surface, letters on a floor). Radius 20–80 px.
  - `floor {mode, color #202024, opacity 0.45 (0–1), z 0}` or a mode alone: `none`, `shadow` (an invisible floor that only catches shadows: an object grounded over a 2D layout), `solid` (a matte plane in `color`), `mirror` (a glossy floor in `color` that reflects the scene; `opacity` is how much of the floor covers the reflection). Put `z` at the bottom of the objects. Shadows need a light that casts them (a sun, spot or point) and `shadows` on.
  - `dof {focusOn?, focusDistance?, fStop?, enabled?}` (`focusDistance` default 1000 px; `fStop` 0.1–128, default 2.8, smaller blurs more; `focusObjectId` is the same as `focusOn`) with `cameraId` (default: the live camera at the playhead; the default camera has none, so add one with `world_camera {fromDefault: true}` first): depth of field through that camera. `focusOn` is a layer kept sharp wherever it or the camera moves (null clears it); otherwise `focusDistance` (px from the camera, along its view). `fStop` 1.4–2.8 for a shallow product look, 8 and above for nearly all sharp. `camera.dof.focusDistance` and `camera.dof.fStop` key (a rack focus); keys win over the value `world_look` writes, and it says so.
  - All of these work on the finished picture: unlit pictures keep their colours, and a transparent world still glows and blurs over the flat layers under it.
  - **Motion blur** works in a world as in a flat comp: the world frame's `motionBlur` must be on (`frame_update`), and the live camera layer's own `motionBlur` (its override) sets the shutter; a moving camera blurs everything it sees. It costs one full render per sample.
- **`world_extrude {ids, depth, bevel?, bevelSegments?, sideColor?, fit?}`** gives pictures in a world thickness: text, shapes, closed paths, images, video and nested frames. `depth` (px) runs away from the camera behind the picture, which stays the front face; `bevel` (px, default 0) rounds the front and back edges over `bevelSegments` (1 to 8, default 3). `fit: "outline"` (the default for vectors and images) follows the real silhouette: the glyphs of text, the outline of a shape or path (holes kept), the opaque pixels of an image (a PNG cut-out); `fit: "box"` makes a slab the size of the picture (the default for video and nested frames). The sides are `sideColor`, or the fill (or text colour) darkened; images get grey sides unless you give one. A picture with thickness takes light (with its material, or a plain one), so light the world. `extrude.depth` and `extrude.bevel` key: depth 0 → 120 grows a logo out of a wall. `depth: 0` makes it flat again. An open path is refused: close every contour with `Z`. Only the fields you give change on a picture that already has thickness.

**Meshes.** `world_add {kind: "mesh", primitive, params?, color?, material?, position, rotation?, scale?}` adds a solid object, lit by the world's lights (a 3D object, unlike a picture, always takes light; `material` is a preset name or values, as in `world_material`). The 13 primitives: `cube` (`sizeX`, `sizeY`, `sizeZ`, `fillet` rounds the edges), `plane`, `grid`, `circle`, `uvSphere` (`arc` below 180 makes a bowl), `icoSphere`, `cylinder`, `cone` (`radius1` base, `radius2` top), `pyramid`, `torus`, `capsule`, `tube`, `torusKnot`. Each stands centred on its `position` with Z up (cylinders, cones, pyramids, capsules and tubes stand upright; planes, grids and circles lie flat like a floor: turn them `rotationX: 90` to face the default camera). Sizes default to 200 px (radius 100); `catalog_list primitives` lists every param with its default and range, and an unknown param is refused. The colour is the mesh's fill (`color`, default #c9cbd1). Every param keys as `geometry.<param>` (a ring that grows, a cone that sharpens, a sphere that opens), and `shading` (`auto` default, `smooth`, `flat`) with `smoothAngle` (degrees, keyable) sets how faceted it looks. To stand an object on a floor at z = 0, raise it by half its height.

**Models.** `world_import_model {localPath | url, frameId?, name?, position?, startMs?}` brings a 3D model file into a world as a mesh: `.glb`, `.gltf` (with its `.bin` and textures beside it), `.obj`, `.stl` or `.ply`, from an absolute path on this computer or an https address (downloaded once). The file stays where it is (the project keeps its path). It goes into the world `frameId` (else the one open in the editor); a flat comp is refused, so make or open a world first. Sizes: glTF keeps its real size at 1 m = 100 px (a 1.8 m figure is 180 px tall), turned so its up is Z; the other formats are fitted to 200 px on their longest side, standing on z = 0 at `position`. Draco and meshopt compressed glTF load. The reply gives `layerId`, `assetId`, `size` (px) and the file's animation `clips`; `world_inspect` lists them under the object's `model`. Scale it with `scaleX`/`scaleY`/`scaleZ` (1 is the size above) and place and key it like any mesh; `world_track`, the look and motion blur work on it.
- *Materials.* A model shows the file's own materials. `world_material {ids, preset}` (or `values`) replaces them with ours on every part (`material.source` becomes `custom`); `world_material {ids, values: {source: "file"}}` brings the file's back (`preset: null` too).
- *Its own animation.* The first clip plays from the layer start, looping. Change it with `layer_set {id, animation: {clip: "Walk", speed: 1, loop: true}}` (`clip: null` holds it at rest; an unknown clip is refused with the list; `loop: false` holds the last pose). The clip follows the layer's own time, so a frame always shows the same pose: trim or move the layer to choose where the action starts, `speed` to stretch it.

**Check before you render.** `world_inspect {compId?, atMs?}` gives the live camera (position, rotation, `fovY`), the environment, the look (bloom, ao, floor) and every object with its world position, rotation and size. Read it first: is the subject in front of the camera, inside the view, and not behind it or too close? Do it again at the end of a move. Then `view_render` for a moment and `view_frames` across the move. In a lit scene, check that a light reaches the subject: a mesh or textured picture with no light on it comes out black or flat, so aim a sun or spot at it, or add an environment or hemisphere light. If you add a floor, its `z` sits at the bottom of the objects (a mesh centred at z = 0 has its base below 0).

**2D to 3D in one shot.** When a flat design should become a world mid-shot, move the very layers that are on screen with `world_move_into` (never rebuild them in a separate world and cut to it: the swap shows), add `world_camera {fromDefault: true}`, aim it with `world_track {lookAt}` at a controller at [0, 0, 0], and key only its position, its first key at the moment of the move holding the default pose. The frame before the move is the flat frame, pixel for pixel; entrances, text animations, effects and the layers' sounds carry on. Camera keys are in world time: comp time minus the nested layer's start. Give thickness or materials before the scene starts or after a cut, not at the move (a lit picture changes colour). Worked script: `references/recipes.md` ▸ "2D to 3D in one shot".

**Recipe: a title flown past by a camera.**

1. `layer_create` the title text in a flat comp, then `world_move_into {ids: [titleId], name: "Title shot"}` (or `world_create {starter: false}` and `world_add {kind: "text", text: "…", position: [0, 0, 0]}`). The layers keep their ids inside the world. The numbers below assume the title stands at the origin.
2. `world_add {compId: <frameId>, kind: "camera", position: [-500, -2200, 120]}`, then `world_track {id: <cameraId>, lookAt: <titleId>}`. It is the live camera at once and stays aimed at the title.
3. `world_inspect`: the title is mid-frame, `fovY` as expected, nothing in front of it.
4. Key the push and slide with `keyframes_set`: `x` −500 → 0, `y` −2200 → −1400, `z` 120 → 0, all `ease-in-out`. No turn keys: `lookAt` keeps the camera on the title all the way.
5. `world_inspect {atMs}` at the start, middle and end, then `view_frames` across the move. The camera's `y` must stay below the picture's (it is at 0), or the camera passes through it.

**Recipes for camera moves.**

- *Orbit:* a camera with `world_track {lookAt: <subject>}`, its `x` and `y` keyed round a circle of radius r (5 keys a quarter turn apart: [0, −r], [r, 0], [0, r], [−r, 0], [0, −r]), then `keyframes_smooth {id}` so the path is a round curve at an even speed.
- *Camera on a controller:* `world_add {kind: "controller", position: <subject>}`, a camera placed and aimed as you like, `world_track {id: <camera>, childOf: <controller>}`, then key the controller's `rotation` (0 → 360, linear, for a full turn). Move the controller to carry the whole shot; add `lookAt` on the camera to keep a moving subject framed.
- *A light riding a car:* the light with `world_track {follow: <car>}` and its position set to the offset it keeps.

**Recipes for the look.**

- *Neon logo:* the logo's paths or text in a dark world (`world_create {starter: false}`, a dark background), `world_extrude {depth: 12, bevel: 2}`, `world_material {preset: "neon", values: {emissionStrength: 4}}`, then `world_look {bloom: {strength: 0.9, source: "emission"}}`.
- *Product on a mirror:* the product (a mesh or a picture with thickness and a material) standing on z = 0, an environment light (`night` or `studio` at 0.3) plus a sun from the side, `world_look {floor: {mode: "mirror", z: 0}, ao: {enabled: true}, bloom: {strength: 0.4}}`, and a slow turntable (`rotation` 0 → 360, linear).
- *A glow over a 2D layout:* `world_create {transparent: true, starter: false}`, the glowing object and a camera, bloom on, then nest the world over the flat design: the glow lands on the layers under it.

**Not in this build yet:** IK, modifiers, sculpting, lattices, mesh editing, line art, faces, shape keys and face poses of models, and physics in worlds. Build depth from models, meshes and pictures: stack text, shapes, icons and images at different `y` for parallax, give logos and titles thickness with `world_extrude`, and light them with `world_material`.

## Undo

Your calls made close together are one undo step for the user, named after your agent app; an edit by the user in between starts a new step. `history_undo` takes back your own newest step and refuses to touch the user's work.
